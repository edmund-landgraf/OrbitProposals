THE OBJECT MODEL

Connected records,
not a document pile.

These are the core business objects that let a proposal carry its context forward.

ORGANIZATION

Who is involved?

Client, provider, users, memberships, roles, and workspace boundaries.

PROPOSAL

What is being offered?

Brief, requirements, sources, options, scope baseline, pricing model, and versions.

REVIEW

Who decided?

Approval steps, comments, flags, review events, meetings, and decision reasons.

CONTRACT

What became binding?

Terms, signature status, billing triggers, payment terms, credits, and retainage rules.

PROJECT

What gets delivered?

Milestones, tasks, time entries, change orders, escalations, and delivery ownership.

AUDIT

What can we explain?

Who changed what, which version was approved, and where an exception entered the system.

THE BOUNDARY

Tools can suggest.
Rules decide.

The assistant can call a tool, but a tool is not permission. Access scopes, workspace roles, server-side authorization, pricing policies, and review requirements remain application responsibilities.

That is why Orbit can be useful to an AI-first buyer without becoming an AI-only system. The same proposal can be inspected by a client, a salesperson, an architect, finance, and delivery.
